Enforcement

What is enforced, and what is not

Every mechanism, what enforces it, how far it has got, and the honest gap. The table is the core repository's, reproduced unedited.

Reproduced from docs/MAPPING.md at bbb20eb. Strength codes are explained in its first paragraphs.

Sovel idea → Rust mechanism

This is the enforcement table the website will reproduce unedited. "Status" says what exists in this repository today; "Strength" says what does the enforcing.

Strength: C = rustc type system · L = lint or tauten check (escapable only by a visible, reasoned #[expect], and not at all where marked "no exceptions") · P = proved for kernel functions (proof tier, planned) · V = convention/review only.

Known gaps

Reproduced from docs/STATUS.md at bbb20eb.

  • The capability rule is discipline, not confinement (see MAPPING.md).
  • The kernel's arithmetic and indexing rules duplicate what an Aeneas proof already establishes (dry-run report). P5 subsumes them only for functions with a recorded, current, total proof. Writfold's adoption branch subsumes 130 of its 159 extracted-kernel findings this way; the other 29 are in functions whose theorems need preconditions (i < len, a cursor invariant, a capacity bound).
  • No credit through callers: a helper with a precondition gets no subsumption even when every caller is proved total.
  • tauten trusts the project's gate output, including its total-shape check; it does not re-check the Lean theorems itself.
  • Any edit under a claimed crate or proofs/ stales every claim of that crate (writfold: a 1.5 h re-run). The workspace manifest (without its version lines) and the toolchain pin count too, since 2026-11-19.
  • Mutant counting differs: writfold's gate counts only direct proof failures, tauten's also counts theorems that depend on a broken one.
  • S002 (placeholder comments) is a heuristic.
  • The benchmark's dev split (4 tasks, one host) hit a ceiling in the pilot. A held-out suite (20 tasks, 3 hosts, per-trial containers) is built on the local branch bench-heldout and kept unpublished until it has run; running it needs the owner's budget approval.
  • #[refined] wraps only tauten::refine::Frozen types (primitives, String, std containers of those): interior mutability or a user-written Clone could break a checked predicate (external review, 2026-11-19). Predicates are arbitrary closures, not a checked fragment.
  • Contracts do not yet support async fn or generic newtypes; they have no Kani (cfg(kani)) lowering yet.
  • Development pilot completed; held-out benefit not established. The dev-split pilot could not separate the arms on escaped defects; nothing here supports a claim that the profile reduces escaped defects.

Out of scope for now

Out of scope for now (a scope decision, not a claim that Rust cannot express them): regions and arena brands, effect polymorphism, captured authority tracking in closures, staged macros, cleanup effects and cancellation protocols. Several have partial Rust encodings (lifetime-branded handles, restricted effect analysis, explicit capability parameters); tauten does not enforce them yet. Benchmark results set their priority; a low frequency in one task suite does not rule a defect class out.
